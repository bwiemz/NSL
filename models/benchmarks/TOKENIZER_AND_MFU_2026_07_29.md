# Tokenizer redesign and pretraining MFU — 2026-07-29

Two measurements, one machine: RTX 5070 Ti (GB203, sm_120, 16 GB, driver
610.43.03), CUDA 13.3, `cargo build --release --features cuda`.

Reproduce with `tools/tokbench/` (tokenizer) and
`models/benchmarks/mfu_bench.py` (throughput). Corpora are generated by
`tools/build_tokenizer_corpus.py` and `tools/build_ood_corpus.py` into
`data/tokcorpus/` (gitignored).

## 1. Tokenizer

### Headline

The same 48.23 MB corpus, pretokenized twice:

| tokenizer | tokens | bytes/token |
|---|---|---|
| GPT-2 design (as shipped) | 12,691,806 | 3.800 |
| two-stage, `transition=40960` | **8,925,916** | **5.403** |

**29.7% fewer tokens for identical input** — a direct reduction in the sequence
positions a pretraining run has to pay for.

### Design

Standard BPE cannot learn a token spanning a pre-tokenizer boundary, so a line
break and the indentation opening the next line are always at least two tokens
no matter how often they co-occur. Indentation is a large and highly
predictable fraction of source text, and that structure is unavailable to the
model.

Training therefore runs in two stages. Stage one uses a cl100k-style word
pattern and learns ordinary subword units. At `transition` the boundary widens
to whole lines and the remaining vocabulary is spent on cross-boundary units,
built on top of a compositional inventory rather than instead of one.
`transition >= vocab_size` reproduces ordinary BPE; `transition = 0` gives
line-level BPE from scratch.

### Evidence

Trained on 48 MB (this repo plus 15 unrelated local projects), vocab 49152,
`min_freq=2`. Three evaluation sets: `nsl` = held-out files from this repo,
`other` = held-out files of projects that were in training, `ood` = every file
of five projects never trained on (a Java desktop client, a CAD tool, a
Python/TypeScript service, a security scanner, a static site).

| design | nsl | other | **ood** | enc MiB/s | nsl/ood |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gpt2 (as shipped) | 3.64 | 4.12 | 3.67 | 60 | 0.99x |
| cl100k | 4.09 | 4.49 | 3.96 | 68 | 1.03x |
| o200k | 4.08 | 4.40 | 3.99 | 63 | 1.02x |
| two-stage `t=40960` | 5.37 | 5.31 | **4.32** | 85 | 1.24x |
| line-level `t=0` | 6.36 | 6.08 | **4.43** | — | 1.44x |

Two things worth noting. Compression improves on genuinely unseen projects, not
only in domain — so this is not purely memorisation. But the in-domain/OOD
ratio rises with relaxation (0.99x → 1.44x), which is why the default stops at
5/6 of the vocabulary rather than going all the way.

A held-out set drawn only from the training repository is misleading here: it
scores line-level BPE at 6.36 B/tok, most of which does not transfer. Any
future tokenizer change should be judged on `ood`.

### Vocabulary shape

Share of the vocabulary containing a newline: 2.1% (ordinary BPE) → 4.9%
(`t=40960`) → 12.2% (`t=0`). Those cross-boundary tokens are the mechanism.

A `max_token_bytes` cap was tried and rejected: at 16/24/32 bytes it costs OOD
compression (4.43 → 4.33/4.36/4.40 at `t=0`) while barely moving the overfit
ratio.

### Bugs fixed along the way

* **No decoder was ever attached.** `Tokenizer::decode` with no decoder joins
  the raw byte-level surfaces with spaces, so `decode(encode(x))` never
  returned `x` — 0 of 158 held-out documents round-tripped. Every tokenizer NSL
  itself produced was affected.
* **`initial_alphabet` was never seeded.** Any byte absent from the training
  corpus was silently dropped at encode time.
* **`nsl_tokenizer_encode_batch` wrote past its allocation.** Rows were emitted
  at natural length and the tensor width inferred by integer-dividing the total
  token count by the batch size; for any ragged unpadded batch that width is
  too small. `make_2d_tensor` then wrote the whole flat buffer into a
  `rows*cols` allocation. Four regression tests now cover this.

## 2. Pretraining MFU

### The roofline, correctly

From the RTX Blackwell whitepaper (App. B, GB203), **dense** TFLOPS:

| | dense | 2:4 sparse |
|---|---|---|
| FP32 vector | 43.9 | — |
| **TF32 tensor** | **43.9** | 87.9 |
| BF16/FP16 tensor, FP32 accum | **87.9** | 175.8 |
| FP8, FP32 accum | 175.8 | 351.5 |
| FP4, FP32 accum | 703 | 1406 |

Memory 896 GB/s, L2 48 MB.

Two consequences. **TF32 tensor peak equals FP32 vector peak on GeForce**, so
NSL's TF32 default buys no roofline headroom — its measured 1.55x GEMM win comes
from the FP32 SIMT path underachieving, not from extra FLOPs. And the marketed
"1406 AI TOPS" is FP4 with structured sparsity, overstating the real BF16
training ceiling by 16x. MFU below is against 43.9 TFLOPS, the peak for the
precision actually running. There is no BF16 matmul path in NSL today.

Coder-50M: 48.77M matmul params, 317.7 MFLOP/token forward+backward at
seq=1024 (`6N` plus the causal attention term).

### Measured

batch=1, seq=1024, `--source-ad`, `NSL_SKIP_GPU_DRAIN=1`:

Each row is cumulative on the one above. Run-to-run spread is ±1.7%.

| config | ms/micro-batch | tokens/s | TFLOPS | MFU |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| as shipped | 1188 | 862 | 0.27 | 0.62% |
| `grad_accumulation=8` | 899 | 1139 | 0.36 | 0.82% |
| + short-axis `sum_dim` | 689 | 1486 | 0.47 | 1.09% |
| + device gather for `dim != 0` | 625 | 1638 | 0.52 | 1.19% |
| + GPU cross-entropy backward admitted | 230 | 4451 | 1.41 | 3.22% |
| + device-resident gradient clipping | 190 | 5389 | 1.71 | 3.90% |
| + GPU embedding backward admitted | 115 | 8904 | 2.83 | 6.44% |
| + cached shape/stride uploads | 102 | 10040 | 3.19 | 7.27% |
| + coalesced `sum_dim` for `inner >= 32` | **81** | **12700** | **4.03** | **9.2%** |

**14.7x end to end; 0.62% -> 9.2% MFU. This is short of the 50–70% target and
section 2.4 sets out, with measurements, exactly what stands in the way.**

> **Measurement trap.** `on_step` fires once per **micro-batch**, not once per
> optimizer step. Verified by running a 65,536-token epoch at batch=1/seq=1024
> with `grad_accumulation=8` and counting 64 callbacks, not 8. Multiplying
> tokens by `grad_accum` inflates throughput by exactly that factor; doing so
> briefly produced a bogus "49.7% MFU" here. `mfu_bench.py` counts per callback.

### The one pathology found and fixed

Kernel profiling attributed **82.5% of all GPU kernel time in a step to
`nsl_sum_dim_f32`**, across only 436 launches — dwarfing sgemm (4.9%) and flash
attention (7.1%).

The general kernel assigns one 256-thread block per output element, which is
right for a long reduction axis. Instrumenting the actual shapes showed the
dominant ones have **`reduce_size == 1`** — a reduction that is a copy —
including 64 launches of `reduce=1 inner=25165824`: copying the 49152x512 tied
embedding gradient by launching **25,165,824 blocks of 256 threads, 6.4 billion
threads to move 25 million floats**. Every thread past `reduce_size` exits
immediately and the survivors still run a full eight-level shared-memory tree
reduction over one live value.

`nsl_sum_dim_short_f32` gives each output element one thread instead. After the
fix the profile is led by real work — sgemm 25.2%, flash attention forward
24.7%, `sum_dim` 20.9% (the genuinely long reductions), and the new kernel
1.1%.

The threshold defaults to 1 (`NSL_SUM_DIM_SHORT_MAX`), because at 2 and above
sequential accumulation reassociates against the general kernel's pairwise tree
and a 20-step run diverges in the 7th significant digit. At 1 the two kernels
are bit-exact — both initialise to `+0.0` and add a single element, including the
`-0.0` case — and a 20-step run matches the general kernel bit-for-bit.

An earlier 20-step comparison did diverge in the last digits at threshold 1. That
was not this kernel: `nsl_embedding_bwd_f32` accumulates the embedding gradient
with `red.global.add.f32`, whose ordering depends on execution timing, and moving
from 25M blocks to 98K changes the timing. **Do not assume `--deterministic`
covers a raised threshold.** That flag substitutes `gpu_det_sum_dim_f32` in
*codegen*, for `sum` written in NSL source only; four runtime-internal callers
reach `gpu_sum_dim_f32` directly (`autodiff/grad_utils.rs`,
`autodiff/backward.rs`, `tensor/ad_ops.rs`, and `flash_attention.rs` for GQA
dK/dV). They are safe at the default of 1 and would not be above it.

### 2.3 What each fix was

Every change is individually gated by an environment variable, and each was
checked against the path it replaces before being measured.

1. **`grad_accumulation=8`** — a config change. Amortises the unfused stdlib
   AdamW chain (74 tensors x 18 kernels) across eight micro-batches.
2. **Short-axis `sum_dim`** — see 2.2.
3. **Device gather for `dim != 0`** (`nsl_gather_dim_f32`, new kernel).
   `nsl_tensor_gather` only had a device kernel for dim 0 and fell back to a
   full CPU round trip otherwise. `cross_entropy` gathers along dim 1, so the
   entire `[B*S, vocab]` log-probability tensor went to the host and back —
   192 MB each way. Bit-identical to the CPU path over 20 steps.
4. **GPU cross-entropy backward** — the kernel already existed and was on by
   default, but its admission gate required targets and logits on the same
   device, and targets arrive from the DataLoader on the host. It declined
   silently, sending 192 MB down and 192 MB back plus a host softmax over 50M
   elements. Uploading the 4 KB target vector instead admits the kernel. This
   single fix was 625 -> 230 ms. A decline now prints the failing condition.
5. **Device-resident gradient clipping** — `nsl_clip_grad_norm` copied every
   gradient to the host for the norm, scaled there, and copied back, with an
   f32->f64 conversion doubling the upload: ~560 MB per step over ~57
   gradients. Now reduces on the device with one batched drain.
6. **GPU embedding backward** — the same admission-gate bug as (4): token ids
   on the host blocked the device kernel, so a `[49152, 512]` scatter ran on the
   host, 96 MB down and 192 MB up.
7. **Cached shape/stride uploads** — the strided-copy path uploaded three small
   i64 arrays per call through the *blocking* `cuMemcpyHtoD_v2`, hundreds of
   times per step. Distinct shapes are few and fixed, so they are cached by
   content. Dropped memcpy calls from 1306 to 250 per step.
8. **Coalesced `sum_dim`** — see 2.2.

Two of these were pre-existing GPU kernels that had simply never run, both
blocked by the same root cause: an admission gate demanding host-supplied
indices already be device-resident. Together they were three quarters of the
total win.

### 2.4 Why the remaining gap is not on the GPU

Sampling `nvidia-smi` during steady-state training shows utilisation
oscillating between 0–11% with brief 97–99% spikes, at ~100 W of a ~300 W
board. Working back from the profile shares, roughly 60 ms of a 649 ms step is
GPU work: **the GPU is idle about 90% of the wall clock.**

Ruled out by measurement, not assumption:

* per-step allocator drain (`NSL_SKIP_GPU_DRAIN=1`) — no effect
* per-launch PTX module hashing — the constants are median 1.9 KB, ~6 ms/step
* `sync_after_kernel` — env-gated, off by default
* **the 72 `.item()` config-scalar syncs.** These were reconnaissance's top-ranked
  item. Direct instrumentation put them at 33.8 us each — 2.4 ms of a 700 ms
  step. Keeping the scalars host-resident so the syncs disappear entirely
  changed the step time by nothing, because the time was spent waiting for GPU
  work that had to happen regardless. A sync probe measures *blocked* time, not
  *caused* time; the distinction matters.
* **larger batch.** Batch 2 gives 4274 tok/s and batch 4 gives 6300 against
  batch 1's 12700. At batch 4 the logits tensor alone is 805 MB, so this is
  memory pressure, not arithmetic intensity. Raising batch needs a
  cross-entropy that never materialises logits.
* **CUDA graphs** — 5.8% when host-bound at 689 ms, no measurable effect at 81 ms.

Still open, and roughly in the order worth attacking:

1. **GPU utilisation is still only ~22-26% at ~60 W of a ~300 W board**, so the
   step remains serialization-bound rather than compute-bound. ~250 blocking
   `cuMemcpy` calls and ~2546 kernel launches per step keep the device
   alternating between busy and idle.
2. **Fused linear cross-entropy.** `crates/nsl-runtime/src/fused_linear_ce.rs`
   contains a complete GPU forward and backward, but the codegen substitution
   that would use it is explicitly deferred ("Sprint 2.5") in
   `stdlib/nsl/nn/losses.nsl`, so `fused_linear_ce` just lowers to the
   composite. Wiring it up removes the `[B*S, vocab]` materialisation entirely,
   which is what currently caps batch size — and batch size is the standard
   lever for arithmetic intensity at this scale.
3. **No BF16 matmul path exists at any flag setting.** That is a factor of two
   on the roofline (43.9 -> 87.9 TFLOPS) that is simply unavailable today.
4. Elementwise and norm ops are one launch each on the training path;
   `nsl_strided_copy_f32` is 8.8% of kernel time over 703 launches, largely
   because GQA pre-expands K and V into full `n_rep` copies.

An honest read: 50–70% MFU on this model and this card needs (2) and (3) at
minimum, plus launch-count reduction. Those are compiler-level projects, not
runtime patches.

### 2.5 Negative results, recorded so they are not retried

* Special-casing `reduce_size == 1` in the short kernel to skip two emulated
  `u64` divides: **slower**, 689 -> 727 ms. The kernel is ~1% of GPU time in a
  host-bound step; the extra basic block costs more than the divides save.
* A grid-strided sum-of-squares with atomic accumulation, replacing the
  single-block reduction in the gradient norm: **slower**, 81 -> 83-85 ms at
  grid caps of both 96 and 1024 blocks.
* Hardcoding the config scalars in the stdlib as an upper-bound probe: **slower**,
  and confounded — turning runtime values into compile-time constants flips a
  fused-kernel admission decision, so it does not isolate the syncs.

Remaining candidates, in the order worth attacking:

1. **72 full device syncs per forward pass** from `.item()` on
   compile-time-constant config fields (`stdlib/nsl/nn/gqa.nsl:48-53`,
   `stdlib/nsl/nn/rope.nsl:43`, `models/coder50m/model.nsl:49`). These scalars
   are stored as Tensors and moved to the GPU by `.to(cuda)`;
   `nsl_tensor_item` syncs only when `device > 0`, so keeping scalar
   non-trainable config tensors host-resident removes all 72.
2. **~2,436 kernel launches per step**, of which ~1,332 were the unfused stdlib
   AdamW chain (74 tensors x 18 kernels). `grad_accumulation` amortises these
   rather than removing them; a fused multi-tensor step removes them.
3. **Allocator churn**: ~2,700 splits and ~2,400 coalesces per step under a
   global mutex, plus ~3 host mallocs per tensor for shape and strides.
4. `nsl_strided_copy_f32` at 12.4% of kernel time over 996 launches — GQA
   pre-expands K and V into full `n_rep` copies to satisfy kernel indexing.
5. **No BF16 path**, which would double the roofline once the host overhead no
   longer dominates.

Batch size is worth noting: batch=4 with accum=8 is *slower per token* than
batch=1 with accum=32 at the same tokens-per-optimizer-step, which points at a
per-batch host cost in the cross-entropy path rather than a compute limit.

### Running any of this

`models/coder50m/pretrain.nsl` loads a Windows path that does not exist on this
machine, and `nsl_load_mmap` aborts rather than warning. Separately, **`nsl run`

## 3. Review outcomes

An independent review of the diff found several real defects, all since fixed:

* **The new PTX kernel was absent from `fused_kernels::ALL_PTX`**, the sole input
  to the ptxas-assembles and pure-ASCII certification gates — so the kernel that
  is now the default path for most `sum_dim` calls was never assembled by CI.
  Registered, and a **drift gate** (`every_ptx_constant_is_registered_for_certification`)
  now fails if any `*_PTX` constant is missing from the table, since the absence
  of such a gate is what allowed it.
* **`NSL_SUM_DIM_SHORT_MAX=0` did not actually disable the fast path** (`0 <= 0`
  is true, and `reduce_size == 0` is reachable for a zero-length reduced axis).
  The escape hatch you would reach for to bisect a suspected miscompile did not
  escape. Now gated on `short_axis_max > 0`.
* **A comment claimed `--deterministic` protects a raised threshold. It does
  not.** That substitution happens in codegen and covers only `sum` written in
  NSL source; four runtime-internal callers reach `gpu_sum_dim_f32` directly
  (`autodiff/grad_utils.rs`, `autodiff/backward.rs`, `tensor/ad_ops.rs`,
  `flash_attention.rs`). Safe at the default of 1; the comment now says so.
* **`special_tokens` would have produced ids past `vocab_size`** — they are
  assigned starting at the model's vocab size, so id `49152` in a
  `[49152, 512]` embedding. Their slots are now reserved before training.
* **`train_from_file` split at every `\n`**, defeating `split_relaxed`'s
  maximal-break-run invariant in the only shipped entry point.
* **`max_token_bytes` counted the byte-level mapping's UTF-8 width**, so a cap of
  8 admitted only 4 spaces of indentation — precisely the runs the cap exists to
  bound. Now counts original bytes, with a test pinning the distinction.
* `assemble` hardcoded the line boundary, so `transition >= vocab_size` was not
  ordinary BPE at *encode* time; `.gitignore`'s `/target/` was root-anchored,
  leaving `tools/tokbench/target` (509 MB) stageable; `make_2d_tensor` now
  `debug_assert`s rather than silently truncating; `encode_batch`'s docs matched
  neither the old nor the new behaviour.

The review also **verified** the new kernel's index arithmetic against the
general kernel line by line, confirmed `reduce_size == 1` is bit-exact between
them (including the `-0.0` case), confirmed the `encode_batch` fix across all
padding/truncation combinations, and confirmed the merge-statistics bookkeeping
is sound including overlapping merges like `[a,a,a]`.

### A negative result worth recording

The review observed that for `reduce_size == 1` the address math is the identity,
yet every thread still executes two **emulated** `u64` divides (no SM has a
hardware integer divide). Special-casing it should have been free speed. It
measured **slower**: 689 → 727 ms per micro-batch against ±1.7% noise, and
reverting restored ~700 ms. The reason is scale — the kernel is ~1.1% of GPU
time inside a step that is ~90% host-bound, so an extra basic block costs more
than the divides save. The special case is deliberately left out, with a comment
in the PTX recording the measurement so it is not "optimised" back in.

### Provenance of the tokenizer figures

`tools/tokbench` originally carried its own copy of the trainer, so the numbers
in section 1 came from a fork rather than shipped code. It now depends on
`nsl-runtime` and calls `tokenizer_bpe::train_two_stage` directly. Re-running
against the shipped trainer reproduces the figures exactly — 5.3714 / 5.3059 /
4.3239 B/tok and 8,925,916 tokens — so every number here is the shipped path.

match the tokenizer's training mix; the default stays at the OOD optimum.

## 5. Code-review fixes (same day, post-campaign)

Two independent review passes over the campaign diff. Fifteen findings were
confirmed against the code and fixed; the notes below cover the ones that
changed behaviour rather than wording.

### The one that mattered most: a certification gate had been switched off

`every_ptx_constant_is_registered_for_certification` was inserted *between* the
existing `#[test]` attribute and `all_handwritten_ptx_is_pure_ascii`. Rust
accepts that with two warnings (`duplicated attribute`, `function is never
used`) and no error, so the ASCII gate silently stopped running — including over
the two PTX kernels this campaign added.

That gate is load-bearing: a single non-ASCII byte in a PTX comment makes
`cuModuleLoadData` fail with `CUDA_ERROR_INVALID_PTX`, and two em-dashes have
already shipped broken kernels for exactly this reason. Verified by injecting an
em-dash into a kernel comment after the fix — the gate fails with the precise
diagnostic, and passes again once reverted.

This is the **fourth** time in this campaign that inserting an item immediately
after an existing attribute or doc block attached it to the wrong symbol. The
same mistake also moved a load-bearing warning about the `std`-vs-`Σx²` confusion
off `gpu_tensor_sum_sq_f32` and onto its new neighbour.

### Correctness

- **Use-after-free in the cross-entropy decline diagnostic.** `tg` was rebound to
  the uploaded targets copy, which the decline path frees before the warning
  block reads `tg.device`/`dtype`/`len`. Reachable on the ordinary 3-D-logits
  path. Diagnostics now capture plain values from the *original* header before
  any upload — which also fixes a second defect: because `tg` had been rebound to
  a device tensor, the "targets are on the host" reason could never print, and
  that was the only reason the upload existed to explain.
- **Determinism leak in the coalesced `sum_dim` route.** The new route admitted on
  `inner >= 32` with no determinism check, on a comment claiming `--deterministic`
  runs never reach the function. The comment 40 lines above says why that is
  false: the flag substitutes `gpu_det_sum_dim_f32` in *codegen* only, and four
  runtime-internal callers reach `gpu_sum_dim_f32` directly. Any broadcast
  reduce-to-shape over `[B, S, D]` has `inner = S*D`, so every such gradient had
  silently lost run-to-run reproducibility. Now gated on `is_deterministic()`.
  `NSL_SUM_DIM_SHORT_MAX=0` also now disables *both* routes, as documented.
- **Stride-blind device grad clip.** Admission checked device and dtype but not
  layout, then read and wrote `data[0..len]` raw. A non-contiguous gradient would
  have its norm taken from the wrong elements and would scale a sibling tensor's
  data. Predicate extracted as `device_clip_admissible` and directly unit-tested.
- **Tied gradients were scaled twice.** The device path scales in place once per
  list *slot*, so a gradient appearing twice — which `@tie_weights` produces, and
  the benchmark model sets `tied_embeddings=True` — got `scale²`. At a typical
  scale of 0.1 that is a silent 10x under-scale. The host path is unaffected
  because it scales an independent copy per slot and writes each back. Scaling
  now dedupes by data pointer; the norm still counts duplicates, matching the host.
- **Stride-blind gather indices.** `nsl_tensor_to_device(p, 0)` is a refcount bump
  for an already-host tensor, so a strided index view was read as if dense by
  `read_index`. Now normalised with `nsl_tensor_contiguous`, as the CPU arm does.
  Also bounded on `gather_dim_size <= 2^24`, since the kernel stages indices as
  f32 and every valid index is below that axis length.
- **Metadata cache vs cuda-graph capture.** Inside a capture region
  `memcpy_htod` defers the payload to graph *launch*, so the cache published a
  pointer to memory that was never written, and skipping the upload on later
  steps changed the recorded op sequence between capture and replay. These
  uploads are immutable and content-addressed, so they now use a new
  `memcpy_htod_immediate` that bypasses capture entirely. The cache is also keyed
  by device ordinal, and allocates from the **persistent** pool: the allocator
  releases *segments*, not blocks, so a never-freed 24-byte block in a transient
  segment would have pinned that segment forever and quietly broken the per-step
  drain.

### Reverted rather than patched

The `len == 1` heuristic that kept model scalars host-resident is **gone**. It
used shape as a proxy for a config-vs-parameter distinction only the compiler
can make, so it also stranded genuine scalar parameters (a learnable
temperature, a `logit_scale`) on the host as f64 — producing a mixed-device
parameter list and a host-resident gradient that makes the grad-clip fast path
decline for the *whole* model, undoing this campaign's own win. It had already
measured no speedup, so there was nothing to weigh against that.

### Where I disagreed with the review

The review wanted `encode_batch` to cap the padded width at `max_len`. That
would silently discard tokens when the caller explicitly passed `truncation=0`,
and independent padding/truncation knobs is what the HF tokenizers this mirrors
do. The non-lossy behaviour is kept; the call now warns once, naming the flag
that gives a fixed shape, so the wider tensor is not a mystery downstream.

### A measured mistake, and its fix

Making the Σx² accumulation exact was straightforward — and the first version was
a **single 256-thread block**, which for the 25M-element embedding gradient is
~98,000 serial f64 FMAs per thread on one SM, at 1/64 rate. It cost **80 → 112
ms** per micro-batch, a 39% step-level regression for a precision concern worth
~1e-5 relative.

The kernel is now grid-strided with one f64 partial per block, summed on the host
in block order (so still reproducible). Measured with the new `NSL_SUM_SQ_BLOCKS`
switch: **1 block = 178.5 ms, 256 blocks = 84.2 ms.** Note this is *not* the
atomic variant tried earlier and reverted for measuring slower — partials go to
per-block slots, so there is no contention and no ordering nondeterminism.

The exactness is worth keeping because the norm feeds a `norm <= max_norm`
comparison: a systematically low sum does not round the answer, it declines a
clip that should have happened.

### The tokenizer work was unreachable from the toolchain

`nsl_bpe_train` — the FFI behind NSL's `bpe_train(...)` — and the `nsl tokenize`
CLI both built their own `BpeTrainer` inline, so none of the trainer work was
reachable from the shipped toolchain. Both are fixed:

- `nsl_bpe_train` now seeds the byte-level alphabet (without it, a byte absent
  from the corpus has no token and encoding silently drops it), attaches the
  ByteLevel decoder, and uses `add_prefix_space=false`. The last one was found by
  the new round-trip test: with a decoder but `ByteLevel::default()`, the
  pre-tokenizer *prepends a space*, so `decode(encode(x))` returned `" " + x`.
- `nsl tokenize` now calls `tokenizer_bpe::train_from_file`, with `--transition`
  and `--max-token-bytes` flags. End to end on `stdlib/`:
  `fn forward(self, x: Tensor) -> Tensor:` is **6 tokens two-stage vs 11
  word-bounded**, and the emitted JSON carries the `Line` split regex plus a
  ByteLevel decoder.

### `assemble` and training could disagree about the boundary

`assemble` re-derived the encode-time boundary from `spec.transition >=
spec.vocab_size` while training compared an *effective* transition against
`vocab_size - special_tokens.len()`. With special tokens reserved and a
transition in that gap, stage two never ran — every merge word-bounded — yet a
`Line` pre-tokenizer shipped, letting word-learned merges fire across boundaries
the vocabulary was never trained on. Training now reports `TrainedBpe.two_stage`
and `assemble` follows it. The gate depends on how full the vocabulary was when
stage one stopped, so it cannot be re-derived from the spec at all.
